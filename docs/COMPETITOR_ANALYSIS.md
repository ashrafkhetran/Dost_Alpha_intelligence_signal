# Competitive landscape

This is a positioning framework, not a current feature/pricing benchmark. Product
packaging and market claims change; verify them from current public sources before
publishing comparative claims or making investment decisions.

| Reference | Category association | Useful design/product question |
|---|---|---|
| Bloomberg Terminal | Professional market information and workflow | How can dense information remain navigable and trusted? |
| AlphaSense | Enterprise search and market intelligence | How should source discovery and evidence traceability work? |
| Feedly AI | Topic/source monitoring and prioritization | How can user-defined monitoring reduce noise? |
| Perplexity Pro | Conversational research and citations | How can an answer lead directly to inspectable sources? |
| Morning Brew Premium | Editorial curation and recurring briefs | What makes a concise daily habit valuable? |
| Linear | Focused workflow UX | How can hierarchy and interaction stay fast and coherent? |
| Arc Browser | Distinctive browser/workspace UX | How can a workspace feel personal without adding clutter? |
| Vercel / Stripe / Notion | Developer and business-product polish | How can status, settings, and premium product details remain clear? |

## Intended differentiation hypothesis

DOST ALPHA may combine topic monitoring, source-backed signals, explicit confidence and
impact definitions, and decision-oriented briefs for AI/business/technology operators.
That differentiation is unproven. It depends on data quality and workflow fit, not just
visual resemblance to premium products.

## Research to complete

For each selected competitor, record research date, source URLs, target segment, data
coverage, workflow, evidence/citation behavior, pricing, export/collaboration, and
limitations. Use only public claims and avoid implying access or equivalence. Interview
prospective users about tools they actually use and what they would replace.

## Decision rule

Do not copy surface patterns without validating the user job. Prioritize cited evidence,
freshness, and low-friction scanning as product hypotheses to test.
